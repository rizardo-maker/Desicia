# DESICIA — from enterprise data to operational action

Phase 1 · strategic and narrative script · 2026-11-27

## Brand positioning
**Category:** enterprise AI engineering partner: analytics, agentic automation and end-to-end transformation.
**Thesis:** Intelligence matters when a business can act on it. DESICIA connects the data, decisions and workflows that enterprises usually treat as separate projects.
**Buyer:** executive and functional leaders evaluating AI across finance, operations, supply chain and customer systems.
**Primary conversion:** Request an architecture audit. A saved audit request is a lead, not a confirmed meeting.
**Secondary action:** Explore the approach (chapter 02).
**Voice:** specific, assured, technically literate. Short statements followed by useful detail. No invented maturity, client roster, ROI evidence or certifications.
**Verbal identity:** signal, decision, action, architecture. No future-tense hype, magical autonomy, artificial urgency or unsupported superlatives.

## Narrative continuity
A single architectural signal lattice moves from dispersed inputs to a connected operating core. Each chapter answers the question left by the previous one. The spatial world is one persistent canvas; the commercial story stays in accessible HTML. Desktop chapters each occupy one viewport; mobile content flows naturally with a static procedural illustration. The final chapter includes the closing footer within its viewport, not an empty extra screen.

## 01 — The signal
**Navigation label:** Overview.
**Eyebrow:** Enterprise AI. Built around your business.
**H1:** Intelligence, / put to work.
**Value proposition:** Connect your data. Automate the work. Build an enterprise that can act on its intelligence.
**Supporting service descriptor:** AI analytics, autonomous workflows and enterprise transformation. Engineered as one connected system.
**Primary CTA:** Request an audit.
**Secondary CTA:** Explore our approach.
**Centerpiece:** a precision brass-and-graphite data lattice, with visible individual signal nodes and architectural rails. Not a brain, globe or unrelated abstract prop.
**Bottom index:** 01 Intelligence / Make your data a decision advantage. 02 Automation / Move from insight to action. 03 Transformation / Build the operating model behind both.
**Handoff:** The lattice's fragmented outer nodes gather into ordered rows as chapter 02 arrives.

## 02 — Unified intelligence / AI-powered analytics
**H2:** See the signal. / Make the call.
**Body:** Bring disconnected enterprise data into one decision layer. See what changed, understand what comes next and give every team a reliable starting point.
**Capability facts, not outcome claims:** cross-system data models; predictive scenarios; decision-ready reporting.
**Interactive plate:** Intelligence console. Three tabs: Revenue, Operations, Supply chain. Each changes the chart, metric, forecast and anomaly rows. All figures are explicitly illustrative and are not claimed as DESICIA client results. A forecast toggle adds/removes the projected path.
**UI sample:** Revenue $4.82m / +12.4% vs prior period; forecast confidence 86%; alerts for collections, pipeline and regional variance. Operations and supply chain use different datasets and units.
**Proof mechanism:** let the executive inspect the model rather than showing a fictitious customer logo wall.
**Handoff:** grid connections become routed paths. Seeing the issue is only half the work.

## 03 — Autonomous execution / AI automations
**H2:** Less handoff. / More momentum.
**Body:** Put AI agents to work across your existing systems. Route decisions, handle exceptions and keep people in control of the actions that matter.
**Interactive plate:** An invoice-processing workflow with Intake → Validate → Policy check → Approve → Post. Clicking any node exposes its role, system boundary and approval rules. “Run simulation” executes a deterministic local demonstration and a timestamped trace; an approval checkpoint pauses until the user approves. Never imply these demo nodes connect to a live ERP.
**Control language:** Human approval where it counts. A trace for every step. Systems you already use.
**Handoff:** routed workflows resolve into a single enterprise core. Individual automations need an architecture that lasts.

## 04 — The transformation blueprint / end-to-end AI transformation
**H2:** A clear path / to AI-native.
**Body:** Start with the business constraint, not the technology. Move from a focused assessment to a governed architecture, a working deployment and a repeatable operating model.
**Method:** 01 Audit — map systems, data and high-value friction. 02 Architect — define boundaries, controls and measures. 03 Deploy — prove one workflow in production. 04 Scale — extend the pattern with ownership and monitoring.
**Interactive scope engine:** enter people in the workflow, hours spent per person each week, loaded hourly cost and recoverable-work percentage. Annual capacity value = people × hours/week × 48 working weeks × cost/hour × recovery fraction. Include a visible formula and conservative defaults. Show weekly hours returned and annual capacity value. These are scenario estimates, not cash savings, guaranteed ROI, a readiness certification or client evidence. No unsupported payback claim without implementation cost.
**Handoff:** the consolidated core stays visible as the page shifts from possibility to deployment diligence.

## 05 — Trust, proof and next step
**H2:** Your next chapter / starts with clarity.
**Body:** Bring us a business bottleneck. We’ll map the data, decisions and systems behind it—and define a practical place to start.
**Conversion:** Request an audit opens an accessible, keyboard-operable dialog. Fields: name, work email, company, priority and optional context. Consent and a honeypot protect the flow. Client and server validate. The API stores a request durably in local SQLite and returns a reference. The UI says “Request saved” and explicitly explains that this local build does not send email or confirm a meeting. Production notification/CRM integration is an environment-configured server boundary; no fake external delivery.
**Concrete proof:** model-derived figures carried from the scope engine, clearly labelled as the visitor's scenario. No case-study claims are available. Explain that a real engagement must establish a measurable baseline and named owner before deployment.
**Enterprise diligence rows:** Private deployment — scope VPC/network boundaries in architecture review. Data handling — agree retention, residency and model-provider terms per system. Security evidence — request applicable controls and independent assurance during procurement. SOC 2 certification, blanket zero-retention and existing private-VPC capability are NOT asserted without verification. These requested topics are addressed as diligence, not fabricated badges.
**Footer:** DESICIA wordmark; Analytics / Automation / Transformation anchors; Privacy opens actual local-build data-use notice; no invented office, phone, social accounts or copyright ownership claims beyond DESICIA site identity.

## Claims and assumptions register
- Supplied by user: name, service categories, enterprise scope, desired WebGL/motion direction and five chapters.
- Proposed strategy: positioning, headlines and audit offer; subject to company approval.
- Demonstrations: all console data and simulated automation traces are synthetic and labelled.
- Calculator: transparent capacity scenario, not guaranteed savings or observed customer outcomes.
- Not supplied: legal entity, domain, address, real contacts, client evidence, pricing, certifications, legal privacy policy, retention obligations, approved deployment/security guarantees.
- Local request retention: stored on the server filesystem; server-side retention is configurable and documented; production must choose a lawful policy and real notification channel before public launch.

## Phase 1 acceptance
Five chapters explain the offer, demonstrate the work, quantify an explicit scenario, address procurement questions and finish with a functioning lead flow. Headlines are intentionally short and must be measured at 1440, 1024, 768 and 390, including the mobile two-line requirement. Business claims must remain truthful even when a visual reference or checker rewards a proof block.
